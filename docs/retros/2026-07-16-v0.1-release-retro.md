## Findings

### What worked well

- **What happened**: All seven declared success criteria were command-runnable, which made this retro's measured-vs-declared pass fully mechanical — including re-running the headless `compound` smoke from scratch with a fresh subagent (deliverable written, terminal signal byte-exact) and re-validating the released commit `0eb72f2` in a temporary worktree with its own 5-check `validate.sh`.
  **Why**: The spec declared each criterion with a concrete proving command (`Measured by` discipline from `designing`'s template), so nothing had to be judged by narrative.
  **How to apply**: Keep every success criterion executable; the ~1-hour-later re-measurement cost was minutes, and it caught zero drift precisely because it could actually run.

- **What happened**: The post-authoring audit of the six distillation drop-lists (`5d9da52`, 00:39) recovered three wrongly-dropped mechanisms — planning's context-research step, scenario flow analysis, and debugging's known-solution check — before the release was declared done.
  **Why**: The drop-lists made every omission explicit and auditable; "judged by 'does any downstream mechanism check the gap this leaves'" (commit message) is a reviewable rubric, not a feeling.
  **How to apply**: When authoring derived artifacts from inventories, keep the drop-list as a first-class artifact and audit it against the finished work — it is the only place where a silent omission is visible.

- **What happened**: Two rounds of independent spec review produced concrete, committed changes — round 1 (`5c6c5df`) reshaped the spec before authoring; round 2 (`8ae3b22`) added the retro interview protocol that this very retro executed.
  **Why**: Heterogeneous-model review (codex) with fresh context surfaces gaps the author cannot see (same mechanism the signal-drift-check retro independently confirmed).
  **How to apply**: The interview protocol exists because a reviewer demanded it; keep review findings flowing into skill text, not just into the reviewed document.

### What to improve

- **What happened**: The loop's own Design gate fired out of order during bootstrapping: all 12 skills were authored by 00:15, spec review round 2 was resolved at 00:56–00:59, and user approval of the design spec was recorded only at 01:05 (`84ee411`) — after implementation was complete.
  **Why**: v0.1 built the process it was supposed to follow; the gates existed on paper before they existed in practice, and a single continuous session made it easy to defer the ceremony.
  **How to apply**: Bootstrapping is the one legitimate exception, but the cost was real — review round 2 became retrofit work (`6bfba75` "Close residual spec drift") instead of pre-implementation correction. The facilitator flagged that the respondent's own "4 commits / ~45 minutes / nothing discarded" framing measures this in commit-cost and hides the gate violation — and that "nothing discarded" is itself unproven, since none of the seven criteria could have detected a discard (three mechanisms *were* silently dropped and only the manual 00:39 audit caught them). Any future skill that changes gate semantics should re-run its own gate order on itself before release.

- **What happened**: All seven success criteria passed at baseline while three distilled mechanisms were silently missing from the authored skills — the criteria are structural (exists / parses / loads / emits contract line) and cannot see content-fidelity drift; the drop was caught only by the separate post-release audit (`5d9da52`).
  **Why**: Presence checks and fidelity checks are different measurement classes; v0.1 declared only the former. The audit that caught the drift existed but was not a declared, pre-release criterion.
  **How to apply**: When a release's core deliverable is content derived from a source inventory, declare a traceability criterion — "every inventory item is present (cite section) or on an explicit drop-list with a reason" — alongside the structural ones.

- **What happened**: Criterion 6's Codex half cannot distinguish discovery paths on this machine: the fresh probe listed all 12 skills, but both plugin-native discovery (`.codex-plugin/plugin.json`) and `~/.agents/skills/` symlinks are simultaneously active, so a clean-install environment was never exercised.
  **Why**: The dev machine doubles as the test environment; the spec even hedged this ("verified at install time, not assumed") without declaring a clean-environment check.
  **How to apply**: Registered as a v0.2-adjacent gap; the first external/clean Codex install becomes the real test — until then criterion 6 is Met only for this machine's configuration.

### Process observations

- **What happened**: The previous retro (same day, 08:44) claimed "No issue tracker or ROADMAP exists in this repo yet" while `ROADMAP.md` had been committed at 00:39 (`5d9da52`) — its two open carry-forward items were therefore tracked in the retro doc alone, violating the retrospective skill's own durable-tracker rule.
  **Why**: Absence was asserted from memory, not checked — the exact failure mode `designing`'s "verify before claiming absence" rule names, recurring inside the skill pipeline's own ceremony.
  **How to apply**: Phase 4's tracker push should start with a one-line existence check (`ls` / `git log -- ROADMAP.md`); corrected this cycle by re-registering both items in ROADMAP.md.

- **What happened**: The authoring source of truth for all 12 skills — the six distillation inventories — lived only in a session scratchpad during the entire v0.1 release, and was preserved into `docs/research/` only the next morning (`7b47fc3`, 07-16 07:29), after the release closed.
  **Why**: The inventories were treated as intermediate scratch, but the release's own audit step (`5d9da52`) depended on them — load-bearing evidence lived outside git for the whole release window.
  **How to apply**: Anything a declared or implied verification step reads should be committed before the verification runs, not preserved retroactively.

- **What happened**: This retro's own interview dispatch exercised the degradation ladder for real: the heterogeneous facilitator (`codex exec`, gpt-5.3-codex) completed round 1 in ~3 minutes with 6 evidence-demanding probes, then hung twice on round 2 (30 and 5 minutes elapsed, 0.09s CPU each — no progress, not slowness), and the interview degraded one tier to a same-family independent-context subagent (opus), which spot-verified cited commits against the repo, accepted 6/6 answers, and produced three observations that materially changed this doc (criterion 6 downgraded to Partially met; "nothing discarded" reframed as unproven; the gate-violation finding strengthened).
  **Why**: `references/dispatch-degradation.md` exists precisely for this; the hang signature (near-zero CPU over tens of minutes on a process that succeeded minutes earlier) was measurable, so the cut-over decision was evidence-based rather than impatience.
  **How to apply**: When a heterogeneous dispatch stalls, check CPU-time-vs-elapsed before waiting longer — one retry, then degrade; the ladder's next tier delivered equivalent rigor (it independently spot-verified commits) at a fraction of the wall-clock.

## Lessons

- All seven structural success criteria passed while three distilled mechanisms were silently missing — presence checks (exists/parses/loads) are blind to content-fidelity drift, and only the explicit drop-list audit could see it.
- "Verify before claiming absence" applies to retros themselves: a retro claimed no ROADMAP existed eight hours after ROADMAP.md was committed, and routed its carry-forward items to the wrong place because of it.
- The pipeline approved its own design spec after implementing it — a process being bootstrapped cannot enforce its own gates on itself, so its first release must be audited as if ungated.
- A success criterion can pass its proving command while being unfalsifiable in the environment it runs in: criterion 6's Codex probe listed all 12 skills, but with two discovery paths simultaneously active the output could not distinguish which mechanism it was certifying.
