## Findings

### What worked well

- **What happened**: The local release packet made four independent claims
  auditable after the ceremony: 4/4 source traceability, the exact three-path
  commit, the annotated tag target, and five green suites. Fresh reruns all
  passed rather than relying on tag existence alone.
  **Why**: The packet bound release intent to committed lifecycle artifacts and
  fail-fast post-commit assertions instead of treating a version bump as
  sufficient evidence.
  **How to apply**: Continue measuring the mapping, file set, commit identity,
  tag target, and validation commands separately in release retros.

- **What happened**: The first real publication classified branch and tag as
  already matching, gated only `page-create`, executed that transition once,
  and then returned `noop/fully-matching` both locally and in an independent
  session. The live page body byte-matched CHANGELOG and was neither draft nor
  prerelease.
  **Why**: Preparation derives a missing suffix from durable state and binds the
  approved packet to complete fingerprints before execution.
  **How to apply**: Record the pre-state classification, exact ordered suffix,
  execution count, and fresh no-op verification for every future live smoke;
  do not summarize publication merely as “release exists.”

### What to improve

- **What happened**: The coordination brief said “stop at the gate.” Even after
  first-hand approval was captured, execution correctly waited for a second
  explicit instruction, although the normal release contract would continue
  immediately on Approve.
  **Why**: The narrower brief imposed a termination boundary, while “gate” can
  otherwise mean a blocking pause that resumes on approval.
  **How to apply**: Future briefs must distinguish “terminate after
  presenting/capturing the gate” from “pause for the gate and continue on
  Approve.” This was conservative scope compliance, not a gate-logic defect.

### Process observations

- **What happened**: Fresh local evidence proved commit/tag/test identities and
  the live no-op; the user separately supplied an independent page/body and
  second-session no-op verification. Both agreed, while the retro keeps their
  provenance distinct.
  **Why**: Combining self-measurement and externally reported state into one
  undifferentiated claim would make review-round evidence impossible to audit.
  **How to apply**: Label local commands, same-session first-hand consent, and
  external verification as separate evidence sources even when they converge.

- **What happened**: The live packet named only `v0.3.0`; local and remote
  `v0.2.0` tag identities remained exact, and no normal or repair action
  targeted the protected release.
  **Why**: The first-smoke row required a new intentionally publishable version
  and explicitly forbade using normal publication to republish `v0.2.0`.
  **How to apply**: Describe this as protected-version non-contact, not as a new
  live rejection test; keep historical v0.2.0 evidence intact while recording
  v0.3.0 as the completed live smoke.

## Lessons

- A release succeeds through mapping, file-set, commit, tag, and fresh-suite
  agreement; “the tag exists” is only one of those proofs.
- A first live stateful smoke is strongest when it mutates only the classified
  missing suffix and a second session independently proves the resulting no-op.
- A gate instruction must say whether approval terminates the handoff or
  resumes execution; “stop at the gate” safely means terminate.
- Protected-version non-contact and a live protected-version rejection are
  different claims and must not share evidence wording.
